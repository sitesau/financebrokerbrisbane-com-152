Browse by Borrowing Purpose

Not all brokers specialise in the same areas. The directory separates providers into categories such as first home buyer brokers, investment loan brokers, and construction and specialist lending. This structure ensures you contact someone familiar with your specific scenario. For example, a construction borrower should look for brokers listed under specialist lending, while a refinancer benefits from a broker focused on switching costs and loan features. The source page separates construction and specialist lending, first home buyer brokers, standard broker listings, investment loan brokers and refinance brokers. That split is more than navigation. It changes what you should ask first. A first home buyer may need a clearer explanation of the loan choice process and the application steps. A refinancer usually needs the discussion centred on switching costs, loan features and whether a change could save money. An investor should treat the enquiry as investment borrowing from the start, because Moneysmart keeps property investment guidance separate from general home loan guidance. A construction borrower should check that the broker is listed under construction and specialist lending rather than assume every broker handles staged or non standard applications routinely.

Check Reviews and Services

Before sending an enquiry, review the details provided for each listing. Look for verified reviews, service descriptions, and contact information. The directory encourages users to compare services and pricing before making contact. This transparency allows you to ask informed questions about fees, lender access, and eligibility requirements. Moneysmart's home loans guidance points borrowers to comparing loan options across lenders, using a mortgage broker, switching home loans, mortgage offset accounts and interest-only loans. That is a useful reminder that a short enquiry should still be specific enough to get a useful reply. Your purpose, buying, refinancing, investing or building. Your timing, whether you are planning immediately or still comparing. The loan features you want explained, such as offset or interest-only options. The area you want the search to cover, if location genuinely matters. The documents or background the broker wants before discussing options. For refinancers, Moneysmart also points to calculating the cost of switching. For investors, its separate property investment guidance is a prompt to ask whether the broker treats the enquiry as an investment case rather than a standard owner occupier loan with a few extra questions added later.

  3. Check reviews. Read verified reviews to gauge local reputation. Look for details on service quality and responsiveness.
  4. Contact directly. Request a quote or call the broker to discuss your needs. Be specific about your purpose and timing.
